Underwritten Public Programs

One organization makes it possible. A community receives it.

An underwritten program is substantive executive work, funded by one organization, delivered for a community or an audience that is not its own. The chamber's members. The university's founders. A public room of executives who would not otherwise be in it.

Three parties

The underwriter

A firm, a bank, a professional-services partnership, a company. It funds the program. What it receives in return, recognition, presence in the room, visibility, how it stands with the community the program serves, is set out as Rights: configured for each program, approved before they are announced, and never assumed. It does not receive the room.

The host

A chamber of commerce, a university, an association, a public body. It convenes the audience and receives a fully funded program at no cost to itself. The room is the host's, and how the host is acknowledged is part of the same approved configuration.

The people in the room

Executives, founders, members. They receive the same work an organization would buy for its own leadership team, and they receive it as participants, not as an audience being marketed to.

The program is Ethan Starke's. The underwriter funds it; it does not shape it.
What this looks like

A firm funds a chamber's leadership series.

Three working sessions across a season for the chamber's executive members, carried by one underwriter across all three.

A bank funds founder places at a university.

Starke Foundry access for a cohort of university founders, underwritten by an institution that has decided those founders are worth backing.

A professional-services firm funds public executive programming.

A public room, a named program, and an underwriter whose recognition is agreed in advance rather than assumed.
